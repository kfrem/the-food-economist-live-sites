21 Essential EPR Questions

The Simple Guide for UK Food Manufacturers & Importers.

1. What is EPR and why does it matter?
EPR stands for Extended Producer Responsibility. It is a new law that changes who pays for recycling.

In the past, taxpayers paid the local council to collect and sort your packaging waste. Now, you (the business that introduces the packaging) must pay the bill. The government wants businesses to be responsible for their packaging from the moment it is made until it is recycled.
2. The Big Money Question: Will this cost me a lot?
It depends entirely on the size of your business:
  • Large Businesses (Turnover £2m+): YES. You will pay heavy fees to cover the cost of collecting and recycling your waste. If you have not budgeted for this, your profit forecast is wrong.
  • Small Businesses (Turnover £1m–£2m): NO. Generally, you do not pay the expensive waste disposal fees. However, you still have to do the paperwork and pay a small yearly registration fee.
3. Who generally has to follow these rules?
You must follow the rules if you meet two conditions:
  1. Your yearly turnover is over £1 million.
  2. You handle more than 25 tonnes of packaging in a year.
Small Producers: (Turnover £1m–£2m OR 25–50 tonnes) = You count and report your data.
Large Producers: (Turnover £2m+ AND 50+ tonnes) = You count, report, and pay the waste fees.
4. When did this start?
The timeline is already active:
  • Data Collection: Started January 1, 2023. You should already have this data.
  • Invoicing: The first bills for Large Producers were sent out in October 2025.
  • Penalties: Higher fees for "bad" packaging (like black plastic) start in 2026.
5. What exactly am I paying for?
If you are a "Large Producer," your bill covers three things:
  1. Waste Management Fees: You pay the local council to pick up household rubbish.
  2. Scheme Administrator Costs: A fee to the government to run this new system.
  3. PRNs (Recycling Certificates): You must buy "evidence notes" from recyclers to prove you met your target.
6. When are the deadlines?
The government is very strict about dates:
  • Large Producers: Report twice a year (by October 1st and April 1st).
  • Small Producers: Report once a year (by April 1st).
Note: If you miss a deadline, your name may be published on a public "shame list" by the regulator.
7. "Deemed Weights" – The Guessing Trap
If you don't weigh your packaging exactly, the regulator will apply a "Deemed Weight" based on standard assumptions.

To ensure they do not undercharge you, regulators may apply conservative deemed weights that can be significantly higher than the real weight — for example, 40% higher or more in some cases.
Rule: Do not let them guess. Weigh your packaging properly to save money.
8. What is "Household" vs "Non-Household" packaging?
This is the most important rule for saving money.
  • Household Packaging: Packaging that could end up in a home bin (e.g., a yoghurt pot). You pay high fees on this.
  • Non-Household Packaging: Packaging that is only used by businesses (e.g., a giant pallet wrap used in a warehouse). You do not pay the high council fees on this.
Action: You must prove which is which, or the government will assume it is all Household and charge you full price.
9. What counts as "Packaging Activity"?
The law looks at what you do with the packaging:
  • Brand Owner: You put your label on a box? You pay.
  • Packer/Filler: You put goods into an unbranded box? You pay.
  • Importer: You bring filled boxes into the UK? You pay.
  • Distributor: You sell empty boxes to small businesses? You report.
10. What if I use Black Plastic?
Black plastic is a big problem. Recycling machines use light beams to sort rubbish, and they cannot "see" black plastic. Because of this, it is on the "Red List."

From 2026, fees for using black plastic are expected to be significantly higher than for more recyclable materials — and could be around double in some scenarios.
Advice: Switch to a different colour or material where possible.
11. I import food from other countries – Is this different for me?
No. There is no loophole here.
If you import a jar of sauce or a box of electronics to sell in the UK, the UK law says you are the producer. You must pay the fee for that packaging. You cannot ask the factory in China or France to pay it for you.
12. Does this apply to Online Marketplaces?
Yes. If you own a website/marketplace that allows businesses from outside the UK to sell to customers inside the UK, you (the marketplace) are responsible for their packaging fees. You must report sales data for those foreign sellers.
13. What is a "PRN"?
PRN stands for Packaging Waste Recycling Note.
Think of it like a "receipt." When a recycler recycles one tonne of cardboard, they print a PRN. You must buy these PRNs to prove to the government that you have paid for your share of recycling. The price of PRNs goes up and down like the stock market.
14. Are Charities exempt?
In many cases, registered charities benefit from exemptions or reduced obligations under these regulations, but the exact position depends on current rules and thresholds. You should always check the latest government guidance or seek advice before assuming that no reporting or fees apply.
15. What about Drinks Containers?
Drinks containers (like plastic bottles and aluminium cans) are treated differently because of the coming Deposit Return Scheme (DRS).

Generally, you report them as "Drinks Containers" in your data. Once the DRS starts, you will pay a deposit fee instead of the standard waste fee for these items.
16. What happens if I make a mistake or don't report?
The Environment Agency has "Civil Sanctions." This means they can fine you without taking you to court.
If you realise you made a mistake, tell them before they find you. If you volunteer the information, the penalty is usually much lower.
17. What is "Nation Data"?
The UK is made up of four nations (England, Scotland, Wales, N. Ireland). The government wants to know exactly where your rubbish ends up.
You must try to report which specific country your goods were sold in. If you don't know, there is a standard formula you can use to estimate it.
18. I am part of a Group of Companies – who reports?
Usually, the Parent Company (the one at the top) must register and report for the whole group.
However, if the subsidiaries act completely independently, they might need to register separately. Check with a consultant if you are unsure.
19. What about Reusable Packaging?
The government loves reusable packaging (like plastic crates that go back and forth).
If you can prove your packaging is reused, you often do not have to pay the waste fees on it after the first use. You must keep evidence that it is actually being reused.
20. How can I lower my bill?
You have three main levers to pull:
  1. Use Less: Redesign your box to use less material. Lighter weight = lower fee.
  2. Switch Materials: Plastic is expensive (~£423/tonne). Cardboard is cheaper (~£196/tonne).
  3. Be Recyclable: Avoid the "Red List." If your packaging is hard to recycle (like black plastic, PVC, or complex laminates), your fees will be much higher.
